# 03 Technique Stations

Status: ready
Time: 13:00–14:00 (60 min) · Talk 14 / do 46
Outcomes: LO4

## Purpose

Add four techniques on top of CRAFT, each matched to a kind of problem, and let
participants discover the difference themselves by running a plain and a
technique version side by side.

## By the end of this block, participants can…

- Ground a prompt in a source and make the AI say when the source is silent
- Use examples to set style and structure
- Break a multi-step task into turns and check between steps
- Get the AI to ask for missing information before it writes
- Say which technique fits which kind of problem

## Talking points: four moves (10 min)

| Move | Problem it solves | Demo line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ground it** | The answer must come from *your* document, not general knowledge | Ask "Does Brookfall reimburse telework internet?" with and without the policy pasted |
| **Show it** | You want a style or structure that's hard to describe | "Here are two past replies in our style…" |
| **Break it down** | Several steps, or the one-shot result is muddled or miscounted | "First list the themes with comment numbers. Stop." |
| **Let it ask** | You don't know what it needs to know | "Before you start, ask me up to five questions." |

Also mention, briefly:

- **"Think it through first"**: asking it to work through the problem step by
  step before answering helps on reasoning-heavy tasks. Many current tools do
  this automatically; it's still worth asking for on hard problems.
- **Roles** ("You are an experienced communications officer…") nudge tone and
  vocabulary, but they're no substitute for real Context. "You are an expert"
  doesn't make it know your policy.

## Debrief chart

Draw it on the flip chart and fill it in from the room:

| When your problem is… | Try |
| --- | --- |
| It made things up / got your rules wrong | Ground it |
| Right content, wrong voice or shape | Show it |
| Big or multi-part task; numbers wrong | Break it down |
| It filled in blanks with guesses | Let it ask |

The usual catches, if the room doesn't raise them:

- **A (plain):** a confident answer about internet reimbursement that isn't
  Brookfall's rule.
- **B (plain):** often promises to "look into reimbursement" or implies the
  City will pay; usually misses Form RM-2 and the 60-day deadline.
- **C (plain):** support/oppose counts that don't add up to 16; sometimes
  repeats the phone number in comment 9.
- **D (plain):** an invented salary, benefits, or degree requirement.

## Check for understanding

Ask 2–3 participants: "Which of these would you use for your own task, and
why?" A good answer names the problem, not just the technique.

## Common problems

| Problem | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Plain and technique versions look the same | That happens with strong models. Ask "what would you trust more, and why?" The grounded one can be checked; the plain one can't |
| Pairs run out of time at a station | Fine. Move on at the call; the debrief fills gaps |
| Station B: the AI refuses to discuss claims | Good behaviour; ask whether it still gave the resident a next step |

## Instructor notes

- Post-lunch energy: get them typing within 2 minutes of sitting down. Have the
  warm-up on screen as they return.
- Station counts differ by run. Don't promise a specific failure; promise a
  comparison.
