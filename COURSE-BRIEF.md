# Course Brief

Status: ready for review

## Title

**Prompt Engineering for Public Service: A First Day with AI**

## Catalog description

Generative AI tools like ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Claude, and Gemini can
draft, summarize, and rewrite in seconds, but only if you know how to ask, and
only if you know when not to. This one-day, hands-on course is for government
employees who are new to AI. You will learn how these tools produce their
answers and where they go wrong, what information you can and cannot put into a
prompt, and a simple method for writing prompts that get usable results on the
first or second try. You will practise on realistic public-sector tasks:
plain-language notices, constituent replies, public-comment summaries, policy
FAQs, and meeting notes. You will leave with a tested, reusable prompt for a task
from your own job. No technical background is required.

## Audience

- **Who:** Government employees at any level (federal, state, county, or city)
  in administrative, program, communications, HR, and frontline roles.
- **Assumed:** Comfortable with email, a web browser, and word processing.
- **Not assumed:** Any prior use of AI chat tools, any coding, any statistics.
- **Why they attend:** Usually some mix of an agency rollout of an approved AI
  tool, curiosity, and worry about getting it wrong. Expect both enthusiasts and
  skeptics in the same room, and design for the skeptics.
- **Size:** 12–30 participants at tables of 3–4. One instructor; a second
  helper is strongly recommended above 20.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the day, participants can:

| ID | Outcome | Taught | Checked by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LO1 | **Explain** in plain terms how a large language model produces an answer, and name three ways that leads to wrong or misleading output | 01 | Pre/post check Q1–3; Activity 1A |
| LO2 | **Decide** what information may go into a prompt and who is accountable for the result, under their agency's rules | 01, threaded through all | Pre/post check Q4–6; Activity 1B card sort |
| LO3 | **Write** a complete prompt with the CRAFT checklist (Context, Request, Audience, Format, Test) that produces usable output for a routine public-service task | 02 | Activity 2A; capstone rubric row 1 |
| LO4 | **Choose and apply** the right technique for a task: grounding in a source, giving examples, breaking the task into steps, or having the AI ask questions first | 03 | Activity 3 stations; pre/post Q7–8; capstone row 2 |
| LO5 | **Verify and refine** AI output against written criteria, one change at a time, and save the result as a reusable template with a human-review step | 04, 05 | Activity 4 log; capstone rows 3–5 |

## Scope

**In scope.** Chat-based AI assistants used through a web or desktop interface.
Writing, summarizing, rewriting, extracting, and brainstorming. Safe and
accountable use. Techniques that work in any major tool.

**Out of scope**, and what to say when asked:

| Topic | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Building chatbots, APIs, agents, automation | "That's a next step. Today is about getting good at the conversation itself." |
| Choosing or buying an AI product | Refer to the agency CIO / IT governance office |
| Legal questions on specific use cases | Refer to agency counsel and records officer; record the question on the parking lot |
| Image, audio, and video generation | Mention it exists; not practised today |
| Fine-tuning, model training, how neural nets work in depth | One slide of intuition only (Module 01) |
| Using AI for decisions about people (eligibility, hiring, enforcement) | Name it as high-risk and subject to agency and legal rules; do not practise it |

## Design decisions

1. **Tool-neutral.** Agencies approve different tools. Every technique taught
   works in ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, and Gemini. Tool-specific setup lives only
   in [instructor/setup-and-tech.md](instructor/setup-and-tech.md).
2. **Fictional data only, all day.** All practice material is set in the
   fictional *City of Brookfall* and lives in [materials/data/](materials/data/).
   Participants never paste real work data in class, even in an approved tool,
   so that the data rule is modelled, not just taught. Their own-task work uses
   made-up details.
3. **Responsible use is a thread, not a module.** Data rules come early
   (Module 01) and then recur in every activity as a "before you paste" check,
   instead of being a lecture at 3:30 when attention is lowest.
4. **One framework, taught once, used all day.** CRAFT is deliberately simple.
   First-time users need one checklist they will actually remember on Monday,
   not a taxonomy of twenty techniques.
5. **Each participant leaves with something they made.** The capstone is a
   reusable template for a real recurring task from their own job.
6. **Hardest new ideas before lunch.** The afternoon is techniques and practice,
   and starts hands-on.
