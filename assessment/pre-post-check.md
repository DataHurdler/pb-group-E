# Pre/Post Check

Status: ready
Used: Module 00 (9:12) and Module 06 (15:45) · 5 min · Individual, anonymous
Outcomes: LO1 (Q1–3), LO2 (Q4–6), LO3–LO4 (Q7–8)

The same eight questions, morning and afternoon. Participants write the same
self-chosen code (for example, the last four digits of a phone number they
know, reversed) on both copies so the two can be paired without names.

Ungraded unless the host agency attaches credit; see
[COURSE-BRIEF.md](../COURSE-BRIEF.md#assumptions-to-confirm-with-the-host-agency).
Answers are in [../instructor/answer-keys.md](../instructor/answer-keys.md#prepost-check).

**My code:** ________   **Morning** ☐  **Afternoon** ☐

**How confident are you that you can write a prompt that gets a usable result
on the first or second try?**  1 ☐  2 ☐  3 ☐  4 ☐  5 ☐

**1.** When an AI assistant answers a question without searching the web, it is
mainly:
a) looking the answer up in a database of facts
b) predicting likely text based on patterns it learned
c) copying the answer from a website it remembers
d) working the answer out from rules of logic

**2.** An AI draft says a requirement comes from "Section 4.3 of the state
procurement code." What should you do first?
a) Use it. AI tools are trained on laws
b) Ask the AI whether it is sure
c) Look up the section yourself
d) Keep the requirement, but remove the section number

**3.** Which task is an AI assistant *most* reliable at?
a) Telling you your agency's current leave policy
b) Rewriting a paragraph you give it at a lower reading level
c) Counting exactly how many of 200 comments mention parking
d) Reporting what your city council decided last week

**4.** Which is safest to paste into your agency's approved AI tool?
a) A resident's complaint email with their name and address
b) A public notice already posted on your agency's website
c) A performance review for someone you supervise
d) Vendor bid prices before the contract is awarded

**5.** You want help replying to a resident email that includes their phone
number and account number. The best approach:
a) Paste it as is, since the tool is approved
b) Don't use AI for this at all
c) Replace the personal details with placeholders, then ask for help
d) Use your personal AI account instead, so it's not on agency systems

**6.** A letter you sent, drafted by AI, gives the wrong deadline. Who is
responsible?
a) The AI vendor
b) Your IT department, which approved the tool
c) You and your agency, as the sender
d) No one; the error was automated

**7.** The AI keeps stating rules that aren't in your department's policy. Which
change will help most?
a) Begin with "You are an expert in our policies"
b) Paste the policy and tell it to answer only from it, and to say when the policy doesn't cover something
c) Ask the same question again
d) Make the prompt shorter

**8.** Which prompt is most likely to produce a usable result?
a) "Write an email about the meeting."
b) "Write a great, professional, clear email about the meeting."
c) "Write a 100-word email to the parks advisory board confirming that the October 28 meeting moves to Room 204. Friendly tone; put the date, time, and room in the first sentence."
d) "You're the best email writer in the world. Write an email."
