# Verify Before It Leaves Your Desk

Status: ready · Outcomes: LO4, safe-use standard

AI writes fluent text whether or not it is right. Fluency is not evidence.
Check in this order: the first items are the ones it gets wrong most often.

## The five kinds of error

| Kind | Looks like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Changed** | A number, date, name, or rule that's different from the source, or reversed | "three months" where the policy says six; "the City reimburses internet" where it says it doesn't |
| **Invented fact** | A detail the source never mentions | "submit receipts to your supervisor monthly" |
| **Invented authority** | A law, policy section, study, or quote that doesn't exist | "Section 9.2 of the FLSA" |
| **Lost caveat** | A "mainly," "may," "estimate," or exception that disappeared | "The City may end an arrangement immediately for performance concerns or operational need" dropped |
| **Overstated** | "Never," "all," "proves," or "caused" where the source is softer | "not permitted under any circumstances" |

## Always check by hand

- [ ] **Numbers, dates, deadlines, amounts.** Compare each one to the source.
  Recount anything counted ("6 of 17 comments…"), and recompute any percentages:
  a change in *percentage points* is not a *percent* change.
- [ ] **Names, titles, phone numbers, addresses, links.** Copy them from the
  source; never trust a retyped one.
- [ ] **Citations: laws, codes, policy sections, studies, court cases.** Look
  each one up. AI tools invent plausible citations.
- [ ] **"Must," "required," "always," "caused."** Overstatement and lost
  caveats are common; the source often says "may" or has an exception.
- [ ] **What's missing.** Read the source side by side. Omissions are harder to
  spot than errors.
- [ ] **Signs it followed the material instead of you.** If pasted text
  contained instructions, did the output obey them?

## Then read it as the audience

- [ ] **Plain language.** Short sentences, common words, the main point first.
- [ ] **Fairness.** Does it make assumptions about people or treat groups
  differently? Would it read the same to every resident?
- [ ] **Accessibility.** Headings, lists, and link text make sense read aloud;
  nothing is carried by color or formatting alone.
- [ ] **Tone.** Would you say this to the person's face?
- [ ] **Promises.** Does it commit your agency to a refund, a date, or an
  outcome you can't promise?

## Last

- [ ] **Disclosure.** Does your agency require you to note AI assistance here?
- [ ] **Would you sign your name to it?** You are.

## AI checking AI: useful, not sufficient

Asking *"List every claim in your answer. For each, quote the source sentence
that supports it, or write NOT IN SOURCE"* catches real errors and is worth
doing. But:

- It misses errors, and it sometimes flags correct statements.
- Asked "Are you sure?", it often changes a right answer to a wrong one, or the
  reverse. **Flipping isn't checking.**
- AI graders favour longer answers, the first option shown, and text that
  sounds like their own.

The same kind of tool that made the mistake is grading it. You still check.
