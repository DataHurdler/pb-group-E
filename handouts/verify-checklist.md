# Verify Before It Leaves Your Desk

AI writes fluent text whether or not it is right. Fluency is not evidence.
Check in this order: the first items are the ones it gets wrong most often.

## Always check by hand

- [ ] **Numbers, dates, deadlines, amounts.** Compare each one to the source.
  Counts are a known weak spot ("12 of 16 comments…"): recount yourself.
- [ ] **Names, titles, phone numbers, addresses, links.** Copy them from the
  source; never trust a retyped one.
- [ ] **Citations: laws, codes, policy sections, studies, court cases.** Look
  each one up. AI tools invent plausible citations that don't exist.
- [ ] **Anything stated as "required," "must," or "always."** Overstatement is a
  common error; the source often says "may" or has an exception.
- [ ] **What's missing.** Read the source side by side. Did it drop an exception,
  a condition, or a deadline? Omissions are harder to spot than errors.

## Then read it as the audience

- [ ] **Plain language.** Short sentences, common words, the main point first.
- [ ] **Fairness.** Does it make assumptions about people or treat groups
  differently? Would it read the same to every resident?
- [ ] **Accessibility.** Headings, lists, and link text make sense read aloud;
  no information carried only by color or formatting.
- [ ] **Tone.** Would you say this to the person's face?
- [ ] **Promises.** Does it commit your agency to something you can't promise,
  such as a refund, a date, or an outcome?

## Last

- [ ] **Would you sign your name to it?** You are.

## Useful, not sufficient

Asking the AI "Check your answer against the source and list anything you
can't support" catches some errors, and is worth doing. It is not verification:
the same tool that made the mistake is grading it.
