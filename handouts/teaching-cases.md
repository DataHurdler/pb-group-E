# When It Went Wrong: Five Real Cases

Status: ready
Used: Modules 01 and 02 · Outcomes: LO1, safe-use standard

Each case is a real, publicly reported event, told briefly, with the lesson
for someone writing prompts. Check the links before
you teach, because some of these stories were still developing when this page
was written (October 2026).

## 1. The brief with six cases that didn't exist

*Mata v. Avianca, U.S. District Court, S.D.N.Y., June 2023.*
Lawyers filed a brief citing six court decisions that ChatGPT had produced.
None of the decisions existed. When the court questioned the citations, the
lawyers stood by them. The judge imposed a $5,000 sanction and ordered them to
notify each judge falsely named as an author.

**Lesson:** the tool invents authorities that look exactly like real ones, and
the professional who signs the document is responsible.
[Opinion (PDF)](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Mata-v-Avianca-Inc.pdf)

## 2. A federal report with studies nobody wrote

*The "MAHA Report," U.S. Department of Health and Human Services and the White
House, May 2025.*
Journalists found at least seven cited studies that did not exist. One was
attributed to a researcher who said she never wrote it. Some citation links
carried markers associated with an AI tool. The report was revised without a
correction notice.

**Lesson:** check every citation, and correct errors openly when you find them.
[Coverage](https://www.ehn.org/white-house-quietly-updates-maha-report-that-included-non-existent-study-citations)

## 3. A contractor's report to a government

*Deloitte Australia, report to the Department of Employment and Workplace
Relations, 2025.*
A government-commissioned review of a welfare-compliance system contained
fabricated academic references and a fabricated quote from a court judgment.
The use of a generative-AI tool was disclosed only in the corrected version, and
the firm refunded part of its fee.

**Lesson:** verify contractor deliverables, and expect AI use to be disclosed
up front.
[Coverage](https://www.techspot.com/news/109758-deloitte-admits-ai-hallucinated-quotes-government-report-offers.html)

## 4. A city chatbot that gave illegal advice

*New York City's MyCity business chatbot, 2024–2026.*
Reporters found that it told business owners they could take workers' tips and
refuse tenants with housing vouchers, both illegal in New York City. It stayed
online for nearly two years after the reporting. In January 2026 the new mayor
moved to shut it down.

**Lesson:** public-facing AI needs testing and accountability before launch,
even when it only gives information. A confident wrong answer from the
government is still the government's answer.
[The Markup](https://themarkup.org/artificial-intelligence/2026/01/30/mamdani-to-kill-the-nyc-ai-chatbot-we-caught-telling-businesses-to-break-the-law)

## 5. City officials' ChatGPT logs, released as public records

*Washington State cities, reported August 2025.*
Public-records requests produced thousands of pages of city staff's ChatGPT
conversations from Bellingham and Everett. In Everett, ChatGPT summarized 29
public comments on the comprehensive plan as revealing "significant concerns"
about growth; a staffer read them and found an overall tone "supportive of more
housing and higher density." Other logs showed invented airport passenger data
(Bellingham), repeated miscalculations for the comprehensive plan, and
references to a state law and a document that don't exist (Everett). In
Bellingham, about half the sentences of a mayoral grant letter matched ChatGPT
output, and none of the records reviewed disclosed AI use.

**Lesson:** your chats may be released, your summaries may be wrong in ways
that matter, and disclosure rules apply. This is Activity 05 in real life.
[Cascade PBS](https://www.cascadepbs.org/news/2025/08/wa-city-officials-are-using-chatgpt-to-write-government-documents/)

## Using these in class

- Tell one case per module, not all five at once. Case 1 belongs in Module 01
  (fabrication), case 5 in Module 02 (records and disclosure), case 4 in Module
  02 (public-facing use needs testing).
- Keep the tone factual. The point is "here's how to avoid this," not "AI is
  dangerous" or "these people were foolish."
- Don't add details that aren't in the linked sources.
