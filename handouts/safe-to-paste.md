## Five questions, every time

1. **Is this tool approved by my agency, for this kind of information?**
   A personal account on a public website is not the same as an
   agency-licensed tool, even if both are called the same thing.
2. **Does it identify a person?** A name, address, phone, email, case or account
   number, or details that point to one person, including coworkers.
3. **Is it restricted?** Confidential, privileged, personnel, medical, tax,
   law-enforcement, security, procurement-sensitive, or under legal hold.
4. **Would I be comfortable seeing this prompt and the reply released?** In many
   jurisdictions, records created for government business, including AI chats,
   can be subject to public-records requests. Ask your records officer.
5. **Could I do this with made-up or removed details instead?** Usually yes.
   Swap "Dana Ortiz, 412 Birch Ave" for "[Resident], [address]."

## Traffic light

| | What | Examples | Do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 **Green** | Public or fictional | Published notices, public web pages, adopted policies, your own generic writing, made-up examples | Fine in an approved tool |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | Internal, not sensitive | Draft internal memo with no personal data, meeting agenda, a procedure you're rewriting | Only in an **agency-approved** tool; remove names first |
| 🔴 **Red** | Personal or restricted | Resident or employee personal details, case files, HR actions, health, tax, criminal-justice data, security details, bid information before award | **Don't**, unless your agency has specifically approved a tool for that data and you've been told so in writing |

## Who owns the output

- **You do.** Whatever you send, file, or publish is your work product, whether
  an AI drafted it or not.
- **AI doesn't decide about people.** Don't use it to decide eligibility, hiring,
  discipline, enforcement, or benefits. Those are subject to legal requirements
  and need a human decision you can explain.
- **Say when it matters.** Follow your agency's rule on disclosing AI
  assistance. When there is none, ask yourself whether the reader would expect
  to know.
