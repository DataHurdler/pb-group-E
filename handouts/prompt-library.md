# Starter Prompt Library for Public Service

Copy, replace every `[BRACKET]`, and remove what you don't need. Each one follows
CRAFT. Before using one with real material, run through
[safe-to-paste.md](safe-to-paste.md).

---

### 1. Plain-language rewrite

> I work in [DEPARTMENT] for [TYPE OF AGENCY]. Rewrite the text between the
> `<text>` tags for [AUDIENCE], at about a [6th/8th]-grade reading level. Keep
> the main point first. Keep every date, number, name, and contact detail
> exactly as written. Use only information in the original. Format: [LENGTH],
> with short headings. After the rewrite, list anything you left out.
>
> `<text>` [PASTE] `</text>`

### 2. Reply to a resident

> Draft a reply to the resident email below, from [OFFICE NAME]. Tone: courteous,
> direct, apologize once if we're at fault, no jargon. Use only these facts:
> [FACTS, PROCESS, TIMEFRAMES]. Do not promise [REFUNDS / OUTCOMES / DATES] we
> haven't confirmed. Under [N] words. End with [SIGNATURE BLOCK]. Here are two
> past replies in our style: [EXAMPLE 1] [EXAMPLE 2]
>
> Email: [PASTE, WITH NAMES REMOVED]

### 3. Meeting notes to action items

> Turn the notes below into a table with columns: Action, Owner, Due date,
> Notes. Use only what the notes say. If an owner or date is missing or unclear,
> write "UNCLEAR" rather than guessing. After the table, list open questions the
> chair should resolve.
>
> `<notes>` [PASTE] `</notes>`

### 4. Summarize public comments

> Below are [N] public comments on [PROPOSAL]. Step 1: list the distinct themes,
> and for each one list the comment numbers that raise it. Wait for me.
>
> *(next turn)* Step 2: write a one-page summary memo for [AUDIENCE] that
> describes the range of views neutrally, notes how many comments raised each
> theme, and quotes no one by name. Mark any comment that is off-topic.

### 5. FAQ from a policy

> Using only the policy between the `<policy>` tags, write [N] frequently asked
> questions [EMPLOYEES / RESIDENTS] would have, with short answers. After each
> answer, cite the section number it came from. If a likely question isn't
> answered by the policy, include it and answer "The policy doesn't say; contact
> [CONTACT]."

### 6. First draft of a memo or briefing

> Draft a [ONE-PAGE] memo to [READER] about [TOPIC]. Purpose: [DECIDE / INFORM /
> APPROVE]. Structure: bottom line up front, background (3 sentences),
> options or key points, recommendation. Use only the facts below. Mark
> anything you had to assume with [ASSUMPTION].
>
> Facts: [PASTE]

### 7. Edit my draft

> Edit the draft below for clarity and concision for [AUDIENCE]. Don't change the
> meaning or add facts. Return the revised text, then a short list of the main
> changes you made and why.

### 8. Procedure to checklist

> Turn this procedure into a step-by-step checklist a new employee could follow.
> Number the steps, one action per step, and flag any step where the procedure is
> ambiguous. Use only what's written.

### 9. Prepare for a meeting

> I'm meeting with [WHO] about [TOPIC]. My goal is [GOAL]. Give me the five
> questions they're most likely to ask and a one-sentence answer to each based on
> the background below, plus two questions I should ask them.

### 10. Let it ask first

> I need to [TASK] for [AUDIENCE]. Before you start, ask me up to five questions
> whose answers would most improve the result. Ask them all at once, then wait.

---

**Not on this list, on purpose:** anything that decides who gets a benefit,
permit, job, or penalty; legal advice; translation of official notices without
review by a qualified human translator.
