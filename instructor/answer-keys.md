## 1A Spot the fabrication

Six planted errors and two omissions in
[flawed-memo.md](../materials/data/flawed-memo.md), against
[telework-policy.md](../materials/data/telework-policy.md).

| # | Memo says | Policy says | Kind | Harm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Eligible after **three months** | **Six months** (§2a) | Changed | Ineligible staff apply; supervisors approve in error |
| 2 | Up to **three days** a week | **Two days** (§3) | Changed | Arrangements approved that violate policy |
| 3 | Agreements valid **two years** | Expire **every December 31**, renew in January (§3) | Changed | Lapsed agreements nobody renews |
| 4 | City **reimburses up to $40/month** for internet | City **does not reimburse** internet, phone, or utilities (§4) | Invented | Staff submit receipts; an expectation of money the City never offered. **Usually rated most harmful** |
| 5 | Out-of-state telework **never** permitted | Permitted with HR Director's written approval, max 10 working days/year (§5) | Changed (overstated) | Staff denied an option the policy allows |
| 6 | Rules follow "**Section 9.2 of the Fair Labor Standards Act**," which "requires public employers to offer telework" | Policy cites no law. The FLSA contains no such provision and does not require telework | Invented | A fake legal basis in an official memo; credibility and possible legal exposure |
| 7 | Ineligible positions: vehicles, public contact | Also **on-site supervision of field staff** (§2) | Dropped | Field supervisors think they're eligible |
| 8 | Either party ends it with 10 days' notice | Also: City may end it **immediately** for performance or operational need (§6) | Dropped | Staff believe they're guaranteed 10 days |

Correct, as distractors: effective July 1, 2025; core hours 9–3; written
agreement; City laptop; in-state requirement; 10 days' notice; HR phone number.
"Satisfactory" for "Meets Expectations or higher" is a softening worth a
mention but not counted.

**Debrief point:** item 6 is the one to dwell on. It is specific, official
sounding, and entirely made up, which is exactly how fabrication looks.

## Capstone exemplar

Show for 60 seconds in Module 05, then take it down so participants don't copy
it.

```
BEFORE YOU PASTE: replace the requester's name, phone, email, and any
account number with [REQUESTER]. Check the dates against the booking system.

I handle picnic-shelter reservations for a city parks department. Below are
our reservation rules (in <rules>) and a request from a resident (in
<request>).

Draft a reply to the request. The reader is a resident who may have never
booked before; friendly, plain language, under 150 words.

Format: first sentence says whether the date they asked for is available
according to [AVAILABILITY: available / not available / alternative dates].
Then the cost, what's included, and how to pay, from the rules. End with
[SIGNATURE].

Use only the rules provided. If the request asks something the rules don't
cover, say "I'll check and get back to you" instead of answering. After the
draft, list anything in the request you didn't answer.

<rules>
[PASTE CURRENT RESERVATION RULES]
</rules>

<request>
[PASTE REQUEST, PERSONAL DETAILS REPLACED]
</request>

BEFORE YOU SEND, I CHECK:
- [ ] Date and shelter match the booking system
- [ ] Fee matches this year's fee schedule
- [ ] Nothing promised that the rules don't allow
```

Why it scores 10/10: all five CRAFT parts; grounding in the rules (the right
technique, since the failure would be inventing fees or policies);
brackets for everything that changes; a before-you-paste line; a Test part
that surfaces unanswered questions; a specific send checklist.
