# Facilitator Guide

Read this once, a week before the day. On the day, use the
[run sheet](run-sheet.md).

## The shape of the day

```
00 first prompt → 01 how it uses context, how it fails → break → 02 what may go in
   → 03 six slots + your task v1 → lunch →
04 ten techniques at six stations → 05 test and refine → break
   → 06 reuse, efficiency, your template, cold test → 07 Monday card
```

The morning gives participants a mental model, the rules, and a structure. The
afternoon adds techniques, testing, and reuse, and ends with something they'll
use on Monday.

**One prompt grows all day.** Each participant's own task is written on an index
card at 8:34, becomes a v1 prompt at 11:42, is re-run at 1:00, gains a technique
at the stations, is tested in Module 05, and is finished between 3:05 and
3:45, with the cold test at 3:25. When you're short of time, protect that thread.

## Conventions

- **Name the failure before the technique.** Every technique answers "what goes
  wrong without it?" Every station runs the plain version first.
- **Never promise a demo output.** Say "let's see," not "watch it fail."
  Variability is curriculum. If the expected failure doesn't happen, ask "would
  you have known if it had?"
- **Read the prompt, not just the output.** In share-outs, put the prompt on
  screen first. The room learns what caused a result, not just that it was good.
- **Ask before you tell.** In live builds, ask the room what to add before you
  add it.
- **Test folklore live, don't argue with it.** If someone swears by "you are a
  world-class expert" or ALL CAPS, run it both ways.
- **Decline "which model is best?"** "Today the thing we control is the prompt."
- **Give watchers a job.** In pairs, one drives and one checks against the
  source. Rotate.
- **Circulate with one question per activity:**

| Activity | Question |
| --- | --- |
| 01 | "Which kind of error is that?" |
| 02 | "What's the least it needs to know?" |
| 03a | "Show me your Audience" |
| 04 | "What did the plain version get wrong?" |
| 05 | "What did you change this round, just one thing?" |
| 06 | "What did your partner have to ask?" |

## What first-time government users need from you

- **Permission to be skeptical.** Many arrive worried about getting in trouble,
  being replaced, or looking foolish. Say early that knowing when *not* to use AI
  is a success condition.
- **Concrete rules.** "Use good judgment" isn't guidance. Point to the traffic
  light and the agency's policy. Where the agency's position is unclear, say so
  and give the conservative default.
- **Mechanics without embarrassment.** Some have never opened the tool at work.
  Normalize asking for help with the interface; it isn't the skill being taught.
- **Their work, not tech demos.** Every example is a public-sector task.

## Difficult moments

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "AI will take our jobs" | Acknowledge, park it, don't debate. "Today is about being the person in your office who uses it well and safely." |
| "This is all hype" | Agree that some is. Ask them to judge by the station results. Skeptics make excellent checkers; give them that role |
| "My agency says we can't use it" | Then today is practice for when it does, and the safety habits apply to any tool. Never encourage workarounds |
| A legal or policy question you can't answer | "I don't know, and I don't want to guess about your agency's rules. It's on the parking lot; you'll get a written answer." |
| Real sensitive data on someone's screen | Privately help them delete the chat and make a stand-in. Never call it out in front of the room |
| A demo produces something offensive or badly wrong | Use it: "This is exactly why you own the output." Show the verify checklist |
| An enthusiast dominates | Make them a table helper with a specific job |
| Mixed experience in the room | Every activity sheet has an "if you finish early" line; point fast pairs to it |
| Questions on ML, agents, governance | Name the series course that covers it and move on |

## Accessibility

- Every handout is plain Markdown and screen-reader friendly; offer digital
  copies.
- The traffic light always carries words (Green/Yellow/Red), never colour alone.
- Read every on-screen prompt aloud.
- Let pairs choose a driver; don't require everyone to type.
- Keep breaks on time; it's a long day.
